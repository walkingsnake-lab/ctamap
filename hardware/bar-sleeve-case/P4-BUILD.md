# CTA LED Board: P4 Bar Sleeve (build sheet, Nov 5, 2026; revised Nov 9)

The v5 drawer bar sleeve for a **Waveshare RGB-Matrix-P4-64x32** (256 x 128 mm), in parts that fit the X2D's 256 x 256 mm bed: a walnut PLA shell in two halves, a black PETG tray in two halves (the print-tested v5 tray from `drawer.py`, cut at the centre), and a two-colour diffuser in two halves (white first layer, black grid). Everything else is the v5 drawer ([README](README.md)), whose fits were settled on test prints (Nov 3). Source: `src/p4.py` (+ the `p4` profile in `src/panel.py`); STLs: `stl/p4/`; every assembly point drawn from the model: `img/p4-assembly-01` to `-10` ([overview](img/p4-assembly-01-overview.png)).

**Revised Nov 9**, before any full print, with what the first P2.5 pair taught ([P3-128-BUILD.md](P3-128-BUILD.md)): a 4 mm wall, a glued plate inside each end wall and a V-groove outside instead of the half-lap and its square reveal, two screws under the knob that hold the top half to the trays, no relief behind the S3's buttons, the pairs' 55° diffuser seat, the diffuser's whole first layer white and its halves built for PETG's shrink, and a longer wall cleat. Of the P4 only the Nov 5 coupons have been printed; the seam and diffuser coupons changed and want printing again, with two new ones.

**Case:** 281.8 x 153.8 x 73 mm (W x H x D), **4 mm wall** (3.5 until Nov 9: ~1.5x stiffer, by thickness cubed). Screen window ~256 x 128 with ~7.4 mm of the diffuser's white border showing round it. The hood is the P3's scaled to the panel (16 mm at the top, 4 at the bottom, vs 12 / 3), so the front's slant matches the P3 (4.5°). Knob 35 mm and collar 41 mm: the v5 parts 18% bigger, for the proportions.

## Decisions
- **Shell split into top and bottom halves** at mid height. Only the two end walls are cut. **The joint is the pairs' (Nov 9; P3-128-BUILD.md, "Joint"), turned onto the end walls:**
  - **Outside, a V-groove** wraps each end (outside, front rim, hood side, back rim): each half's visible edge at the joint is chamfered 0.5 mm x 45°, and the halves meet in its bottom. A V has no ledge for a seam blob to sit on, and reads as one deliberate line.
  - **Flat end faces, hollowed:** each half's two end faces are relieved 0.3 mm except a 2 mm land along the visible edges, so two not-quite-flat printed faces still close where you see them. Both of a half's faces lie in one plane, so they are sanded flat together on glass before gluing.
  - **A glued plate inside each end wall:** a rebate 1.3 mm deep in the wall's inner face straddles the joint, 46 mm tall and 49 mm along the depth (from behind the rim's seat to 2 mm in front of the tray's back). A flat PLA plate (`p4-joint-plate.stl`, 49 x 46 x 1.2) lies in it, half in each shell half, **glued to both**: 11 cm² in each half, 22.5 cm² an end, PLA to PLA. Its face sits 0.1 mm below the bore, so the trays never touch it (their side crush ribs run 7 mm outside it and bear on walnut). 2.7 mm of wall is left behind it; on the right end the rebate stops 5.5 mm above the USB-C opening. Printed back-down the rebate is a pocket in a vertical face whose front end slopes 45°, so it has no roof.
  - **Why not the half-lap** this replaced (the inner half of the wall as a 1.9 mm lip on the top half, into a pocket in the bottom half; its coupon fit Nov 5): it is the joint that stepped on the first P2.5 pair, its lip is the kind of thin walnut feature every pair joint that failed had, it gives 1.8 cm² of glue an end against 22.5, and its seat can't be dressed flat. Two things had ruled a plate out here, and both went Nov 9: the wall was 3.5 mm (a rebate would have left 2.2), and the relief behind the S3's buttons sat right under the joint on the right end. `JOINT=lap` still builds it, and its seam coupon is exported beside the plate's for a comparison. **Neither joint has been printed at full size on any build**; the plate joint's coupon is the first print to make.
- **The trays hold both shell halves (Nov 9).** The two screws from below thread into the tray halves through the bottom half; nothing but the glued joint and the crush ribs held the top half. Now **two M2.5 x 8 button heads under the knob**, 15 mm either side of its axis, go down through holes in the collar's flange and the top wall into a boss on each tray half, built onto the outside of each encoder rail. The tray halves cross the shell's joint, so with both shell halves screwed to them they splint it; the 274 mm top wall is tied down at mid-span; and knob presses go into the trays. Each head sits on the walnut, flush with the flange top, 1.0 mm under the knob (press travel 0.5); a 0.2 mm pad on the tray under each takes up the clearance so the screw doesn't dish the wall. M3 doesn't fit: its head would show past the knob. Checked by `src/check_p4_top.py`: screw tips 1.27 mm above the encoder's QT plugs, the bosses pass 0.82 mm over them as the tray slides in (the rails' own clearance), 3.95 mm of thread in the PETG, the heads 0.15-0.45 mm inside the knob's edge seen from above. Two things to know: each head bears on only ~6 mm² of walnut (snug, not tight), and 1.55 mm of walnut is left between the knob hole and each screw hole (the collar's crush ribs are turned 15° so none presses there).
- **Wall 4 mm, no relief behind the S3's buttons (Nov 9).** The relief (a v1 leftover, from when the board sat against the wall) thinned the right end wall to 0.8 mm over 13 x 23 mm directly under the shell joint, above the USB-C opening. In the drawer the board's edge sits 3.15 mm inside the wall's inner face, behind the tray's own wall (which is open in front of the buttons), so a button would have to stand 3.15 mm past the board's edge to reach the walnut: the wall is now full thickness there. **USB-C:** the board, and so the port, is where it was (the relief never set it); with the thicker wall the port is 7.15 mm behind the outer face (6.65 on the 3.5 mm P3 drawer). A plug seats because its housing goes in through the 13.65 x 8 mm opening, which passes the largest housing the USB-C spec allows (12.35 x 6.5, also checked at 12.85 x 7.0) with 0.47 mm or more round it, through the tray's window, to 0.5 mm from the board (`src/check_p4_fit.py`).
- **Diffuser seat: 55°, 1.0 mm overlap (Nov 9)**, the pairs' (P3-128-BUILD.md, "Diffuser seat"): rim 1.5 mm in from the bore, the flange's bevel 0.2. The v5's 45° seat holds the flange by 0.3 mm, a wedge that let the diffuser push through the rim of the first pair's long walls. Printed back-down the seat is a 55° overhang, which no shell has printed yet: the seam coupon includes it.
- **Diffuser in two halves**, split on the grid rib between pixel columns 32 and 33. That rib is 1.2 mm (0.6 a half; the others 0.8). The 8 mm flange is the border round the pixels. **The whole first layer is white** (the face and the flange's front, as the single builds; until Nov 9 the flange's front was black); everything above it is black, so the print has one filament change, after the first layer. **Built 0.45% large for PETG's shrink (Nov 9):** the first P2.5 pair's black PETG grid printed that much small, which on a 128 mm half is ~0.6 mm each way, enough to pinch the skirts onto the panel and walk the ribs off the gaps between the LEDs. `DIFF_SHRINK` in `p4.py` (0 for a PLA grid); the shrink strip below measures your filament's. **Glued into one piece** before it goes on the panel: nothing else holds the centre joint closed (each half is located only by its own outer skirt), and a key across it would either print with an overhang or show in the bezel.
- **Tray split at the centre**, so its seam crosses the shell's. It's `drawer.tray()` with its split options, so it carries every v5 fix: posts with ±0.7 mm of screw float, 0.3 mm long; pilots instead of nuts; encoder rails; the sensor sled; the status window printed in. The seam's edges are chamfered 0.5 mm where the halves pass each other (the right half's leading edges, the left half's back edge), so the right half can't catch on the left as it slides in. Each half bolts to its two corner inserts and takes one screw from below. A **bridge** (bottom) screws into the panel's bottom-centre insert through a notch half in each tray half; a **spine** (top) bolts across the seam into the cleat's two bosses. On a wall, the French cleat replaces the spine.
- **S3 on the right tray half, stock ribbon to the panel.** Plugged straight onto the panel's data in, the S3's USB-C edge lands ~1 mm past the bore and its side buttons would leave under 1 mm of wall. It sits between the right-hand posts, 5.5 mm higher than in v5: its STEMMA QT plug enters from the board's bottom edge, and at v5's height it would land on the lower-right post (the wall behind its edge buttons has no relief any more, above). Its ribbon connector is **22 mm below** the panel input: one pair of ribbon hooks on each tray half, the left pair at the panel input's height and the right pair at the connector's, and the ribbon folded twice at 45° between them.
- **Panel turned 180°** (data in on the left seen from the front), as in v5. The top-centre insert isn't used (the spine ties the top of the seam).
- **Wall cleat 220 mm (Nov 9).** The case cleat is the single build's (150 mm; it bolts to the spine's two bosses, one in each tray half, so on a wall it ties the tray seam as the spine does). The wall cleat is longer, with three wall screws (centre and ±95 mm): the single's 149 mm left 66 mm of this case overhanging each end, now 31. It ends 5.7 mm short of the Boot funnel on the tray's back and prints flat. The rim sits 8 mm off the wall, as before.
- **M2.5 screws: guide bores and pre-threading (Nov 9).** On the P3 builds the small screws were hard to start and keep straight (the S3's four above all): a blunt machine screw in a bare Ø2.4 pilot has only a 0.5 mm chamfer to guide it and can lean until two or three threads have bitten. So the first 1.5 mm of the S3 posts' pilots and of the sled's front-screw pilot is now a Ø2.6 guide bore (`M25_GUIDE_L` in `drawer.py`, set by `p4.py`; 0 on the printed P3 builds): the screw drops in and stands square before it cuts. Thread left below it: 4.8 mm in the S3 posts (of 6.4), 3.6 mm in the sled's boss (of 5.2); 1.7 mm of post wall round the bore. The top screws' bosses get no guide bore (3.95 mm of thread is all there is): their lead-in went from 0.4 to 0.6 mm (3.35 mm of full thread below it), and they are **pre-threaded on the bench** before the trays go in, as are all the M2.5 pilots (Assembly). The Ø2.6 bore is unprinted: if it comes out too tight for a screw to drop in, the screw still starts centred; if too loose to steady it, lower `M25_GUIDE_D`.
- **Cables modelled:** `src/p4_cables.py` places every cable and plug seated (the ribbon with its folds and slack, both IDC plugs, the power lead, its lugs and the tied-up spare, the QT plugs and runs, USB-C), from Adafruit's S3 board file and typical catalogue sizes, and checks them against every part: no overlaps. The lengths below come from it.

## Before printing: measure and test
1. **Insert check:** `check-p4-corner-inserts.stl` (any PLA, 250 x 124, flat). Flip it onto the panel's back: an M3 should drop into all six inserts at once (corners x ±120, z ±56; centres x 0, z ±56, from the drawing). The corners take the tray halves, the bottom centre the bridge. If not, correct `inserts`/`standoffs` in `panel.py` and rebuild.
2. **Insert depth:** seated, the M3 x 8 flanged button heads reach ~5.0 mm into the inserts (5.3 before the posts' 0.3 mm settles). Check one goes in 5.3 mm without bottoming.
3. **Panel thickness**, LED face to the back of the housing: the model assumes 14.5 (12 mm housing per the drawing + 2.5 board). The posts are 0.3 mm long on purpose, so a panel up to 0.3 mm thinner still sits on the diffuser. A thicker one pushes the tray back, and past +0.4 mm the screws from below miss the shell's slotted holes (±0.4). Outside −0.3 / +0.4 mm, set `t` in the `p4` profile and rebuild.
4. **Data-out header** (behind the S3 once the panel is turned): lay a ruler across the housing back over it. It may stand at most ~1 mm proud of the housing: the S3's bottom socket passes right over it, 1 mm above that plane.
5. **Remove the magnetic screws** from all six inserts if fitted (they stand ~2 mm proud; the posts and the bridge bolt there).
6. **Coupons.** Nov 5: the seam, drawer-fit and diffuser-joint coupons fit as printed. Since then (Nov 9) the seam and the diffuser changed, so those two want printing again, and there are two new ones:
   - `coupon-p4-seam-top.stl` + `coupon-p4-seam-bottom.stl` (walnut, back-down as exported, 5 mm brim: they stand 69 mm tall on a 5.5 mm edge) + one `p4-joint-plate.stl` (PLA, flat): the **left end wall's whole joint at full size** (hood, rim, V-groove, hollowed faces, the whole rebate, back rim), where the Nov 5 pair was only the front 34 mm, which can't show a step along the depth. Sand both faces flat on 220 on glass until the land is bright along its whole length. Lay the plate in one half's rebate and bring the other on: the plate should drop into both rebates with a little play and sit flat (binds: `JP_CL` up to 0.15 in `p4.py`; proud of the bore: it must sit below it), and the land close with no gap along the outside and the front. Look at the rim's back slope, the 55° diffuser seat: it should be clean, not drooped (if it droops, `SEAT_DEG` 50). Then glue the pair with its plate as below, finish it (lap, oil), and try to twist and pull it apart by hand.
   - `coupon-p4-seam-lap-top.stl` + `coupon-p4-seam-lap-bottom.stl` (optional): the same piece of wall with the half-lap instead. Glue it the same way and break both: whichever holds is the joint (`JOINT=lap python3 p4.py ../stl/p4` builds the lap shells).
   - `coupon-p4-diffuser-shrink-white.stl` + `-black.stl` (new; one object, two parts, face down, the diffuser's settings; ~137 x 18 mm): the bottom strip of a diffuser half. It is built 137.22 mm long and should measure **136.6**. If it measures m, set `DIFF_SHRINK` to 1 - m / 137.22 and rebuild (`DIFF_SHRINK=0.003 python3 p4.py ../stl/p4`). Print it in the filaments the halves will use.
   - `coupon-p4-diffuser-joint-{left,right}-{white,black}.stl` (two-colour, face down): glue the two at the joint as below and hold them on the panel at the bottom centre. The ribs must sit between the LEDs and the joint fall on the column gap.
   - `coupon-p4-top-screw-shell.stl` (walnut) + `coupon-p4-top-screw-tray.stl` (black PETG) (new; both stand on their back edge as exported, with a brim): one top screw's stack. Drive an M2.5 x 8 through the walnut into the PETG: it should thread in firmly (pilot `TS_PILOT` 2.4, the sensor sled's) and pull the two together with no gap and no dent round the head. Do it as in the build: first drive the screw into the bare PETG piece and back it out (pre-threading), then start it blind through the walnut: it should find its thread without leaning.
   - `coupon-p4-drawer-fit-shell.stl` (walnut) + `coupon-p4-drawer-fit-tray.stl` (black PETG): unchanged in fit (the bore didn't move); the crush ribs should press in with firm hand force, no wobble (tune `CR_H` / `TRAY_CL` in `drawer.py`).

## Print settings
- **Shell halves:** Bambu PLA Wood profile, filament dried (~8 h at 50-55 °C) and flow-calibrated. **fixed 0.16 mm layers, no variable layer height** (it banded the P2.5 pair's halves differently, and the two bands would meet at the joint), 5 walls on a 0.4 nozzle (the 4 mm wall prints solid), elephant foot 0.15. **Brim 3 mm with a 0.1 mm gap, or brim ears, not 5 mm:** the halves sit diagonally with 4.5 mm a side. Keep the 45° turn they come with (don't let auto-arrange rotate them back). Seam **Aligned**, scarf seam off; in Preview check it isn't on the joint faces' land (the outer 2 mm), and paint it onto an inside back corner if it is (a blob on the land holds the halves apart; one in the hollow or in the rebate's corner is harmless). The diffuser seat prints as a 55° overhang: leave overhang slow-down on. Z hop 0.6 mm. Plate washed with dish soap, then glue stick; bed 55-60 °C; door closed. The bed's corners have to print well: if unsure, print the seam coupon at a bed corner first.
- **Tray halves:** PETG, 0.4 nozzle, 0.24 mm layers, 4 walls, 4 top + 4 bottom layers, 15% gyroid, monotonic bottom surface, textured PEI, elephant foot 0.15, Z hop, dried PETG (the v5 tray's settings). The right half's window part takes the translucent PETG: the swaps happen only in its first ~4 layers.
- **Diffuser halves:** 0.4 nozzle, first layer 0.2, all white (the face and the flange's front), 0.2 layers after it all black (one filament change), first layer 30 mm/s, monotonic bottom surface, elephant foot 0.
- **Spine, bridge:** PETG on the tray plate at its 0.24 mm layers (the bridge's floor is cut for them); 100% infill on the spine. **Collar:** 100% infill (translucent PETG glows evenly that way). Small parts (key, sled): print beside a larger part.

  - 2 x M3 x 10 **countersunk** (spine, into the cleat bosses' pilots). Not in the Hantof kit either.
  - 2 x M3 x 10 button head (from below, threaded into the tray's pilots).
  - 2 x M2.5 x 8 button head (top screws, under the knob; in the Hantof kit).
  - 4 x M2.5 x 8 (S3 onto its posts, self-tapping).
  - Light sensor (optional): 2-4 x M2.5 x 5 (sensor onto the sled), 1 x M2.5 x 6 button head (sled, from outside the tray; a socket cap would stand proud and jam the tray).
  - The encoder's M7 nut and washer.
- **Wall hanging instead of the spine:** 2 x M3 x 10 (case cleat into the same pilots) + 3 x #8 / 4 mm countersunk wood screws or anchors (the 220 mm wall cleat).
- **Electronics:** Waveshare P4 panel (its power cable and 30 cm 16-pin ribbon are used); Matrix Portal S3 (5778); I2C QT rotary encoder (5880); optional VEML7700 (4162, flat).
- **Cables:**
  - Ribbon: the panel's 30 cm one. The P4 needs ~23 cm seated (the 22 mm drop adds to the P3's route), ~1 cm more while the right half rests on the case's back in step 7 and ~3 cm more while you line it up behind its slot in step 9 (on the bench beside the case it would need ~30-33 cm and a twist); the P3's 15 cm minimum doesn't apply. Measure yours: at least 26 cm between the plug faces, or use a 40 cm 16-pin HUB75 ribbon.
  - STEMMA QT: S3 → sensor **30 cm** (its route is 17-19 cm: a 20 cm only reaches laid dead straight; the spare folds behind the S3) and sensor → encoder 15 cm (it spans ~9.5 cm in step 7's pose, ~4 cm seated); or one **40 cm** S3 → encoder without the sensor (~24 cm seated, but ~32 cm in step 7's pose, where its encoder end is plugged: a 30 cm won't reach; its spare folds behind the S3 too).
  - A data-capable USB-C cable and a 5 V / 3 A USB-C supply.
- **Other:** 4 round black bumpers 12.7 x 3.8 mm (3M SJ5312 size); 1-2 hook-and-loop ties ~12 mm wide (power cable bundle, through the tie loop); 30-minute two-part epoxy for the shell's joint plates and the diffuser joint (Devcon 2 Ton: no bloom, time to line things up; one 0.84 oz syringe does both); packing tape; isopropyl alcohol.
- **Tools:** hex keys 1.5 mm (M2.5) and 2 mm (M3; its long arm has to reach ~34 mm down the post tubes and ~37 mm down the bridge's); pliers or a small wrench for the M7 nut; a 2 mm (or 5/64") drill bit, turned by hand; a flat sheet (glass or stone tile) for the glue-ups; 220 and 400 paper; four small spring clamps with ~45 mm of reach (the joint plates) and tape or a strap round the case; toothpicks and a scrap of card to mix the epoxy.
- **Power:** the firmware doesn't cap the current. Full white across the P4 (e.g. a calibration screen) can exceed a 3 A supply; the transit screens draw well under 1 A.

## Gluing and finishing the shell
1. **Flatten the joint faces.** Tape a sheet of 220 paper to the glass. Stand each half on its two end faces (they lie in one plane) and sand in short strokes until the 2 mm land is bright all along both ends; the hollow behind it stays matte. Blow the dust out of the rebates.
2. **Dry-fit**, back-down on the flat sheet: a plate in each of the bottom half's rebates, the top half down over them. The back rims are one plane, so the halves can't step front to back; the plates line the end walls up, in and out. Check the land closes with no gap along the outside and the front, the V is one even line on both ends, and a tray half slides through.
3. **Glue** with the 30-minute epoxy (CA would bloom white inside the V-groove, where it can't be sanded). Wipe the rebates, the plates and the lands with isopropyl alcohol. A thin, even coat on each plate's glued face (the one that printed on the bed) and a thin line on the lands, kept 1 mm back from the V so nothing squeezes into it. Plates into the bottom half's rebates, top half on, packing tape on the sheet under both ends, tape or a strap round the case to pull the halves together, and **two spring clamps on each plate from the back opening**, one on each half, a scrap of card under each jaw: they press the plate onto the rebates' floors, which is what lines the two walls up. Wipe any epoxy that stands proud of the bore before it sets (the plate sits 0.1 mm below it; the tray passes with 0.25 mm). It sets in ~35 minutes and reaches full strength in 8-12 hours (overnight), quicker warm (~25 °C; not over 40 °C, the walnut PLA softens in the 50s). Then slide a tray half through each end to check.
4. **Deburr** the knob hole, the USB-C opening and the two screw holes underneath. Light-sensor pinhole: it prints ~Ø1.9 with a slight sag at the top; twist the 2 mm bit through by hand from the outside, then break the edge with a larger bit turned between the fingers.
5. **Lap the front flat** on 220, then 400, paper taped to glass, in figure-eights, across the joint so both halves end flush. Clean the V-groove with a folded strip of paper. Scrape the print seam, sand along it with a flexible pad, and feather.
6. **Finish:** scuff the outside evenly (grey Scotch-Brite or 400 wet), then Danish oil or wax. Mask the bore, the knob hole, the USB-C opening, the foot recesses and the two screw holes first. Oil goes on after gluing: glue won't take the oil, so keep it off the outside. Lay oily rags flat to dry. Once the finish has cured, twist the 2 mm bit through the sensor pinhole by hand to clear it.

## Assembly
The panel goes in first; the encoder after it (its board hangs where the diffuser's flange passes); the left tray half, then the right one; the bridge and spine; the two top screws and the knob last.

**Before anything goes in, pre-thread every M2.5 pilot on the bench** (1.5 mm key): the four S3 posts and the top-screw boss on the right tray half, the top-screw boss on the left half, and the sled's boss and sensor holes if fitted. Drive an M2.5 x 8 into each bare pilot, sighting it square from two sides, until it is ~5 mm in, then back it out (top-screw bosses: from the pad on the tray's top, until the tip shows under the boss). The screws then follow a cut thread instead of starting one behind a board or through the walnut. Later, start each screw by turning it backwards until it clicks down into the thread.

1. **Shell:** bumpers into the four recesses underneath; press the collar into the knob hole (its crush ribs make it a firm push).
2. **Diffuser (the evening before):** a strip of packing tape on the flat sheet under the joint (the epoxy won't stick to it). Wipe the centre rib's faces with isopropyl alcohol, then spread a thin film of the epoxy with a toothpick on the back half of each half's rib face only (none near the face, so any squeeze-out goes to the back). Lay both halves face down, push their straight edges together at the centre and hold a straightedge along the top and bottom skirts so the halves line up. Leave it overnight, then snap the diffuser onto the panel (skirts over the panel's edges).
3. **Panel cables, on the bench:** the ribbon into the panel's data in (red stripe = pin 1) and the power lead into its power header.
4. **Panel in:** shell face down on a towel. Panel turned 180° (data in on the left seen from the front), in from the back until the diffuser's flange seats on the rim. Lead the ribbon and the power lead out of the back toward the USB-C end, clear of the bore's walls, so the left tray half can't trap them.
5. **Encoder (flip the shell):** lay the shell top-down across two supports with the knob hole between them and the case's depth level; check the panel is still fully forward. From inside, drop the encoder into the collar's pocket, shaft first, NeoPixel toward the front, its board's edges square to the case's sides. Gravity holds it while you thread the M7 nut and washer on from outside and snug it. Turn the shell back over **face down or onto its side, never onto its back** (the panel could slide out). Leave the knob off until step 13.
6. **Left tray half:** slide it in from the back and push it fully forward. Drop 2 x M3 x 8 flanged button heads down its post tubes (with plain button heads, an M3 washer down each tube first) and start them **loosely**, a few turns. Its rail passes the encoder board's left edge; if the half stops there, the board is turned: square it as in step 9. Reach in on the USB-C side and press the ribbon into the left half's hooks: it runs along the back wall at the panel input's height, its free end toward the USB-C end.
7. **Right tray half, front up, resting on the case's back over the left half** (shell still face down; the half turned end for end, its open seam edge along the case's centre, so the cables run straight up out of the open right side into it):
   - Light sensor (optional): the VEML7700 chip up onto the sled, 2-4 x M2.5 x 5 down from the chip side. Plug in the 30 cm QT cable (from the S3) and the 15 cm one that will go to the encoder. Tilt the sled about 10° (front down), put its two back tabs into the two hooks on the top louver band, swing the front up to the top wall and drive one M2.5 x 6 from **outside** the tray's top wall (the counterbore beside the light hole).
   - **Lay the S3's QT cable first,** flat on the tray's back wall where the S3 will cover it: from just above the lower-right post, along under where the board's bottom edge will be to its middle, straight up, then over to the sensor (30 cm), or on to the encoder (40 cm) without the sensor. Fold any spare flat on the back wall there.
   - Plug the ribbon into the S3's IDC header, that QT cable into its STEMMA QT socket (bending the wires straight back off the plug) and the power leads into its screw terminals **while the S3 is loose**: on its posts all three face the tray's back wall, and the QT socket (on the board's bottom edge, near the USB-C end) sits just above the lower-right post. Then lower the S3 onto its posts over the cable, 4 x M2.5 x 8 from its panel side: each drops 1.5 mm into its post's guide bore and stands square; start all four a turn or two before driving any home. Take the power lead off the S3's left edge, not across its front.
   - Leave ~9 cm of the power lead free from the panel's power plug, then fold the rest in ~3.5 cm lengths and tie it through the tie loop. The free length is what lets the half rest here and line up in step 9; once seated ~5 cm of it lies loose along the back wall.
   - Ribbon: between the two hook pairs it drops 22 mm. Fold it twice at 45° (crease lightly) where it leaves the left half's hooks, and clip it into the right half's hooks; fold any slack flat (a Z, creased lightly) on the right half's back wall between the seam and its hooks, keeping just enough to reach: a standing loop of 6-8 cm would reach the panel's back. Keep the ribbon flat where it crosses the seam line.
8. **Encoder cable:** plug the QT cable's free end (from the sensor, or the S3) into the encoder's socket on the USB-C side (right-hand seen from the front, on your left looking in from the back), under its board, reaching in through the USB-C side **now**: it can't be reached once the right half is in. Let the cable drop straight down from the plug, not along the board's edge: the rails slide in just above it. Seated, ~11 cm of this cable is spare: as the right half goes in (step 9), guide its loop down into the tray, away from the right rail.
9. **Right tray half in:** every cable inside the tray's walls (none over its top, bottom or end walls: the crush ribs would trap them). Lining it up behind its slot takes ~3 cm more ribbon than seated: let the Z fold open, and it takes the slack back as the half goes home (a slight bow at the seam is fine). Slide it in and push it fully forward. Its rail passes the board's right edge (the left half's is already beside its left edge); if it stops there, the board is turned: pull the half back, loosen the M7 nut a turn, square the board and snug the nut (the knob is still off). 2 x M3 x 8 flanged button heads down its post tubes, started loosely.
10. **Bridge:** its post in through the notch at the bottom centre; 1 x M3 x 8 flanged button head, started loosely.
11. **Spine** across the top: push the two halves together at the seam, then 2 x M3 x 10 countersunk into the cleat bosses' pilots (countersunk heads have no float: the spine sets the halves' spacing while the post screws are loose). On a wall, the case cleat instead ([Wall mount](README.md#wall-mount-optional)). Now snug all five post screws (2 mm key: the long arm down the tube, turned gently by the short arm; the heads bear on narrow printed floors).
12. **From below:** 2 x M3 x 10 button heads into the tray's pilots. The first time, drive each in slowly, back it out once, then drive it home and stop when the head seats (snug, not tight: the head bears on the walnut).
13. **Top screws:** 2 x M2.5 x 8 button heads down through the two holes in the collar's flange, either side of the encoder's nut, through the top wall into the tray halves' pre-threaded bosses (1.5 mm key). Turn each backwards until it clicks into the thread, then drive it. Snug, not tight: each head bears on a narrow ring of walnut. Then push the knob on until it stops on the shaft tip; it hides both heads.
14. **Firmware:** the panel sits turned 180°: set the board up with **Upside down** (the control page's Set up a board, or `scripts/setup-board.js --rotate 180`), or `MATRIX_ROTATION 180` in `secrets.h` for a setup build. If its colors look wrong, set it up again with the color order it needs (firmware-s3 README, "Wrong colors"). Plug in USB-C.

- **Flashing:** hold the Boot key in through the funnel on the back, plug in USB-C, upload, replug.
- **Placement:** on a shelf, keep ~1 cm behind the case: pushed flat against a wall the rim's 4 mm recess seals both louver bands. Or hang it on the cleat (8 mm off the wall). Keep it out of direct sun (dark walnut PLA softens at ~55-60 °C).
- **After a week:** re-snug the encoder's nut once.
- **Service:**
  1. Unplug USB-C. On a wall, lift the case off the wall cleat first, and work on the bench.
  2. Pull the knob off and take out the 2 top screws. The 2 screws from below; the spine (on a wall build, the case cleat's 2 screws instead: it ties the halves the same way); the bridge.
  3. The right half's 2 post screws, then draw it out: thread an M3 a few turns into its cleat boss's pilot to pull on (it's above the half's centre, so rock it out). Rest it front up on the case's back as in step 7 (S3 and sensor come with it, still cabled) and unplug the encoder's cable once you can reach it.
  4. **Free the right half before the left one:** resting there it covers the left half's screws and blocks it. Take the S3 off its posts (4 x M2.5) and unplug the ribbon from it, unplug the power lead at the panel's power header (reach in through the open right side), and lift the right half away.
  5. The left half's 2 screws; unclip the ribbon from its hooks, then draw it out the same way.
  6. To get the panel out, flip the shell top-down, take the encoder's nut off (the knob came off in step 2) and lift the encoder out first.

## Watch for
- **Heat:** typical draw (1-3 W) is fine. Sustained full white at daytime brightness can push the inside toward ~55 °C in a warm room; run the S3's chip-temperature log once, on the brightest screen, where the board will live.
- **Top screws:** snug only; each head bears on ~6 mm² of walnut, and overtightening dents it. They, and the two from below, are what hold the shell halves to the trays: don't run the case without them.
- **Crush ribs:** each tray removal shaves them a little. If a half ever feels loose, raise `CR_H` in `src/drawer.py`.
- **Light sensor:** it sees the room through the Ø2 pinhole, and the firmware's lux end points are guesses: tune them from the serial log ([firmware-s3 README](../../firmware-s3/README.md)).

## Open
Facts not measured yet (each changes the model or the sheet):
- **Not printed since the Nov 9 revision:** the plate joint, its V-groove and the 55° seat (seam coupon; the plate joint has not been printed on any build yet), the top screws' thread, blind start and bearing (top-screw coupon), the Ø2.6 guide bores in the S3 posts and the sled, the diffuser's shrink in your filaments (shrink strip: 0.45% assumed, from the P2.5 pair's black PETG).
- **USB-C reach:** the port is 7.15 mm behind the outer face, 0.5 more than on the P3 drawer; any plug whose housing fits the 13.65 x 8 opening reaches it.
- **Panel thickness:** 14.5 mm assumed (see "Before printing").
- **Insert positions and depth:** the check frame and the depth test cover them. Also whether all six insert boss faces sit level with the housing back (the bridge assumes it), and which inserts carry magnets.
- **Header positions and heights:** from a photo, ±1 mm. They set the hook heights, the ribbon's route and the data-out clearance.
- **Panel outline:** 256 x 128 assumed; the diffuser's skirts have 0.15 mm of clearance.
- **Ribbon length:** 30 cm assumed.
- **The P4's power cable:** whether its ends fit the S3's terminals, its length, and how far the power plug stands off the housing.
- **The P4's peak current**, against a 3 A supply.
- **The diffuser's centre joint:** how it looks with the panel off depends on the white face, and how well the epoxy holds this PETG; glue and flex the joint coupon before the full print.
- **PETG shrink:** how much the tray halves shrink sets the seam and the spine's fit. The fit coupon shows it.
